# I. Governing Object and Doctrine

## 1. Object, field, theory, and completed treatise

- **Reditus** is the object: admissible return through collapse under declared conditions.
- **Structura Reditus** is the field that studies Reditus.
- **Generative Collapse Dynamics (GCD)** is the foundational theory of generative collapse and admissible return inside that field.
- **Summa Reditus**, Architecture Freeze v1.0, is the completed canon-facing treatise and whole-work return of the corpus architecture.
- Other papers, casepacks, closures, specifications, protocols, diagrams, teaching works, and technical artifacts remain local witnesses unless explicitly recontracted.

Completion is architectural, not exhaustive. Future proof, closure, case, computation, translation, teaching, edition, and application burdens remain open.

## 2. Governing axiom

> Collapse is generative; only what returns through collapse is real.

> *Collapsus generativus est; solum quod per collapsum redit, reale est.*

Operationally:

> Collapse generates. Return admits. Collapse alone does not earn return credit.

## 3. Publishing doctrine

Every public work must enter under a declared object, role, burden, boundary, and repair route.

The governing publishing law is:

> Do not publish discovery as resolved structure. Publish a declared local object under the burden it can actually carry.

The one-line protocol is:

> Publish by burden. Describe by role. Cite by source boundary. Repair by declared seam.

## 4. Corpus return and local proof

*Summa Reditus* returns the corpus as one ordered body. That corpus-level return does not make every local theorem, closure, computation, casepack, seam, teaching claim, or publication claim conformant.

Each local work remains answerable to its own:

- object and contract;
- mandate and non-goal;
- source and evidence boundaries;
- closure rules;
- missingness policy;
- return and seam conditions, where active;
- publication and version record;
- derived stance, where evaluation is requested.

Publication preserves a public boundary. It does not replace proof.

# II. The Two-Axis Architecture

## 5. Authority axis

| Tier | Primary authority | Publication consequence |
|---|---|---|
| **Tier-1** | Preserves kernel meanings, reserved symbols, exact identities, primitive distinctions, and canon-fixed boundaries. | A paper may explain, cite, compute, or apply Tier-1. It may not redefine Tier-1 locally. |
| **Tier-0** | Governs contracts, adapters, validators, closures, ledgers, manifests, run records, seam accounting, audit, and stance derivation. | A protocol, validator, gate, ledger row, or stance rule requires declared adoption and validation. |
| **Tier-2** | Translates domains, readers, datasets, language surfaces, diagnostics, and candidate extensions into bounded form. | A closure or translation may enable ingress. It may not become canon, proof, adopted protocol, return, seam, or stance by usefulness alone. |

Shortest complete form:

> Tier-1 defines. Tier-0 executes and audits. Tier-2 translates and prepares. No tier may silently borrow the authority of another.

## 6. Functional axis

| System | Function | Boundary |
|---|---|---|
| **UMCP** | Measures and evaluates declared objects. | Measurement and evaluation do not create Tier-1 meaning. |
| **RCFT** | Explores candidates before freeze. | Recurrence, elegance, simulation, and discovery do not canonize a candidate. |
| **ULRC** | Preserves meaning across language, translation, pedagogy, public explanation, and domain movement. | Fluency, clarity, or contact do not create proof or stance. |

Authority is not function. Tier-1, Tier-0, and Tier-2 are not names for UMCP, RCFT, and ULRC.

## 7. Frozen-run rule

Inside a frozen run or frozen manuscript contract:

- Tier-2 improvement does not retroactively change the active closure;
- a diagnostic does not become a gate because it later proved useful;
- a candidate does not become canon because it gained contact;
- a language repair does not rewrite a prior stance;
- an implementation habit does not become Tier-0 protocol by repetition;
- a later source does not silently alter the original source boundary.

New insight may create a new candidate object, amended contract, repair note, or declared seam. It may not silently rewrite the frozen object.

# IV. Publication Roles

## 11. Role rule

Every public work has one primary job. It may contain many secondary insights, but it enters the corpus under one primary role and one primary burden.

If a draft is trying to carry two primary jobs at full strength, split it, recontract it, or reduce one burden to secondary status.

## 12. Role types

| Role | Primary job | Governing limit |
|---|---|---|
| **Ingress paper** | Opens a durable route into the corpus. | It does not replace the completed treatise or force every reader through the archive. |
| **Map paper** | Shows the architecture at a glance. | It orients; it does not prove every technical claim. |
| **Core paper** | Declares a canon-facing object, field, theory, hierarchy, or primary distinction. | It does not silently absorb every local proof or implementation burden. |
| **Pass paper** | Walks one object through the operational spine. | It demonstrates traversal; it is not the whole theory. |
| **Closure paper** | Translates one domain or case family into evaluable form. | It does not redefine Tier-1 or replace the domain's native science, art, law, language, or method. |
| **Casepack** | Demonstrates a bounded case burden. | It witnesses local range; it is not universal proof. |
| **Canon update** | Declares a repair, hierarchy shift, weld, or mature correction. | It must state what changed, what remained, and where the seam lies. |
| **Companion or method paper** | Improves routing, pedagogy, use, or interpretability. | Support is not constitutional authority. |
| **Technical reference** | Preserves protocol, validator, repository, schema, ledger, or implementation discipline. | Executable or procedural authority does not become field declaration or Tier-1 definition. |
| **Language paper** | Preserves meaning across translation, Latin, pedagogy, domain movement, or public explanation. | Fluency and semantic repair do not bypass evidence or stance. |
| **Critique paper** | Handles refusal, boundary, contact, disagreement, or public accountability. | Critique must declare its object and evidence boundary; unavailable structure may require NON_EVALUABLE. |
| **Teaching paper** | Transmits the method to another evaluator. | Course architecture does not prove learning efficacy without assessment evidence. |
| **Publication note** | Places a public work for readers. | Placement and metadata do not prove the local claims of the work. |

Role is assigned by burden, not title, prestige, chronology, length, DOI status, or rhetorical force.

# V. Discovery and Publication Modes

## 13. Discovery mode

Discovery mode is appropriate for:

- notes and scratch derivations;
- RCFT exploration;
- candidate invariants, mappings, closures, and diagnostics;
- unresolved terminology;
- source comparison;
- simulation and external-contact pressure;
- speculative synthesis;
- internal outlines and repair proposals.

Discovery outputs must retain their actual status: exploratory, candidate, diagnostic, translation draft, bridge candidate, proposed validator, source note, or blocked object.

Discovery does not create canon, adopted protocol, closure completion, return, seam, proof, or final stance.

## 14. Publication mode

Publication mode begins only when:

- the object and role are named;
- mandate, non-goal, reader result, and primary claim are stable;
- scope and exclusions are frozen;
- source, authority, evidence, code, and publication boundaries are declared;
- missingness is classified;
- required closures are known;
- active thresholds and tolerances are declared rather than inferred;
- open seams are recorded;
- the work can complete its local burden without exploratory drift.

## 15. Mode-transition rule

The transition from discovery to publication is a functional handoff, not automatic promotion.

No public work may discover its own primary purpose while presenting itself as finished. If its purpose changes materially during drafting, return the work to discovery mode, recontract it, and declare any resulting object or version seam.

The handoff record must state:

```text
Source function: [RCFT, ULRC, UMCP, or other]
Receiving function: [declare]
Object handed off: [declare]
Source burden closed: [declare]
Source burden not closed: [declare]
Receiving burden: [declare]
Authority status: [declare]
Missingness transferred: [declare]
No-promotion clause: Handoff does not promote authority by itself.
```

# VI. Claim Budget

## 16. Standard budget

Unless a role-specific contract declares otherwise, use:

- one primary claim;
- up to three supporting claims;
- one decisive example or witness;
- one decisive failure mode, limit, or countercondition.

This is a drafting default, not a universal Tier-1 constant. A work that needs a different budget must declare the reason before drafting expands.

## 17. Memory test

Every paper must be reducible to one sentence:

> This paper will be remembered for ______.

If the sentence cannot be completed without listing several unrelated achievements, the object or burden is still diffuse.

## 18. Evidence-budget rule

Claim strength may not exceed evidence burden.

- A conceptual claim requires a definition, distinction, admissibility condition, or interpretive boundary.
- A formal claim requires a formula, proof, derivation, identity, theorem statement, or appropriate formal source.
- A computational claim requires code, data, manifest, validator behavior, run record, or reproducible computation.
- A closure claim requires declared observables, adapter, weights, thresholds, missingness policy, return domain, source boundary, and authority boundary.
- A seam claim requires return typing, ledger reconciliation, residual, tolerance, frozen contract, and declared seam policy.
- A corpus-architecture claim requires placement, burden mapping, hierarchy, and boundary preservation.
- A publication claim requires version, date, DOI record if assigned, license, extent, citation surface, and repair route.
- A teaching-efficacy claim requires learner artifacts, assessment records, and repair evidence; a syllabus alone supports only a bounded architecture claim.

Meaningful but unproven material must remain typed as conceptual, diagnostic, exploratory, illustrative, architectural, or non-evaluable.

# VII. Canon Passport

## 18A. Relation-to-object and kernel-sameness readback

Publication role, authority, function, canon placement, and relation-to-object are distinct descriptors. A work may be Tier-2 in authority, ULRC-facing in function, canon-facing in placement, and concerned with kernel preservation without becoming Tier-1.

### Relation-to-object labels

Use only when material to the active burden.

| Label | Governing question |
|---|---|
| **Identity** | What authoritative object is held fixed? |
| **Realization** | Does this form recover the declared object or burden? |
| **Preservation** | Does a change retain every distinction required by the burden? |
| **Concordance** | Do different forms recover the same burden output? |
| **Fiber equivalence** | Do two forms distinguish exactly the same admitted alternatives? |
| **Translation** | Can one form reconstruct another? |
| **Implementation identity** | Is this the same concrete technical artifact/version? |
| **Provenance** | Where did the artifact or representation come from? |
| **Historical continuity** | Is it legitimately the same evolving historical object? |
| **Reditus** | Did the object return admissibly through collapse under the relevant history and contract? |

Do not infer a stronger relation from a weaker one.

### Burden-relative determination

For a representation

```latex
E:X\to Y,
```

write `ker E` for its equality-fiber relation. This is not the UMCP kernel.

For an exact burden signature `F_B`, determination through `E` holds iff

```latex
\ker E\subseteq\ker F_B.
```

Equivalently, if `C_E` is the off-diagonal collision set of `E` and `D_B` is the set of pairs distinguished by the burden,

```latex
C_E\cap D_B=\varnothing.
```

Loss alone is not failure. Retention alone is not adequacy.

### Repair and deterministic restatement

```latex
U_B(E)=C_E\cap D_B.
```

A repair must separate every unresolved decisive pair. If `G=g\circ E`, then `G` is deterministic restatement of the existing record and cannot split an already merged fiber. New form is not new evidence.

### Heterogeneous articulation

For `E_i:X\to Y_i`, individual sufficiency is

```latex
\ker E_i\subseteq\ker F_B,
```

and joint sufficiency for a retained family is

```latex
\bigcap_i\ker E_i\subseteq\ker F_B.
```

Exact deterministic translation `E_i\to E_j` exists on realized images iff

```latex
\ker E_i\subseteq\ker E_j.
```

Burden concordance is weaker than exact translation.

### Kernel identity and realization

**Kernel identity** is reserved for the already-authorized Tier-1 object: reserved meanings, exact identities, primitive distinctions, and canon-fixed boundaries required for the kernel to remain itself.

For readback only, let `F_K` denote an analytical signature of that already-authorized canonical kernel burden. `F_K` is not a new Tier-1 symbol.

A representation or implementation `E` realizes the kernel burden when

```latex
F_K=q\circ E
```

for an admissible readout `q`. Equivalently,

```latex
\ker E\subseteq\ker F_K.
```

A realization may preserve surplus information.

### Kernel preservation and concordance

Kernel preservation requires

```latex
C_E\cap D_K=\varnothing.
```

Two realizations are kernel-concordant when

```latex
F_K=q_1\circ E_1=q_2\circ E_2.
```

Kernel concordance does not require equal realization fibers.

Fiber equivalence,

```latex
\ker E_1=\ker E_2,
```

means the forms distinguish the same admitted alternatives. It is not canonical semantic identity. An invertible recoding can preserve fibers while violating reserved meanings.

Therefore:

```text
fiber equivalence != canonical kernel identity
Tier-1-facing != Tier-1 authority
```

### Historical burdens remain separate

Implementation identity, provenance, historical continuity, and Reditus remain distinct. Endpoint agreement does not prove any of them.

### Validation boundary

A finite validator establishes only its declared tested surface unless the admitted domain is finite and exhausted or a proof supplies universal coverage. Do not promote finite observed concordance into universal kernel equivalence.

### 20.5 Reditus condition

For a bounded trace under frozen contract:

```latex
\Psi(t_0)\to\Psi(t_1)\to\Psi(t_R),
\qquad t_R>t_1.
```

Reditus occurs only if the candidate state re-enters the declared return domain under the same contract across the collapse-return boundary.

```latex
\Psi(t_R)\in\mathcal{D}_{\Phi}(t_R),
\qquad
\Phi_{\mathrm{before}}=\Phi_{\mathrm{after}}.
```

```latex
\operatorname{Real}_{\Phi}(\Psi)
\iff
\operatorname{Adm}_{\Phi}(\Psi)
\land
\tau_R<\infty_{\mathrm{rec}}.
```

Return is admissible re-entry, not repetition, persistence, resilience, restoration, resemblance, recovery, or rhetoric.

### 20.6 Seam and weld rule

When continuity is claimed, the manuscript must name the single active Tier-0 accounting profile. This publishing protocol does not canonize a universal return-credit estimator.

At minimum, the active contract must distinguish:

```latex
\mathrm{Credit}_{\mathrm{Reditus}}
=
\text{admissible return credit defined independently by the active contract},
```

```latex
\Delta\kappa_{\mathrm{ledger}}
=
\kappa(t_1)-\kappa(t_0)
=
\ln\!\left(\frac{IC(t_1)}{IC(t_0)}\right),
```

and any declared debit terms such as:

```latex
D_{\omega},\qquad D_C.
```

For an additive seam profile using these terms, the residual is:

```latex
s
=
\mathrm{Credit}_{\mathrm{Reditus}}
-
\bigl(\Delta\kappa_{\mathrm{ledger}}+D_{\omega}+D_C\bigr),
```

with closure only when:

```latex
|s|\leq\mathrm{tol}.
```

If return is typed as infinite recurrence, return credit is zero:

```latex
\tau_R=\infty_{\mathrm{rec}}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\mathrm{Credit}_{\mathrm{Reditus}}=0.
```

The reconciliation term must be independently observed or supplied under the frozen contract. Do not define `Delta_kappa` from the same return-credit and debit terms that the residual is supposed to test; that makes closure algebraic rather than evidentiary.

If a manuscript uses an inherited `R * tau_R` profile, a `Q_R` profile, or another estimator, it must declare the estimator, units, sign convention, provenance, timing, censoring behavior, validators, source version, and authority status. Profiles may not be mixed within one run or silently treated as Tier-1 identities.

Finite return does not by itself establish continuity. The seam must reconcile independently.

- **NONCONFORMANT:** violates the active contract.
- **NON_EVALUABLE:** lacks sufficient structure for a verdict under the active contract.

Stance derives last. Regime, diagnostic force, DOI status, citation count, public clarity, and author confidence are not stance.

State object, role, mandate, non-goal, reader result, primary claim, scope, exclusions, and corpus relation.

### 2. Canon passport

Carry the minimum passport. Add the expanded operating passport only to the degree required by the work's active burden.

### 3. Local object declaration

State exactly what is new or repaired here: a closure, theorem family, pass, casepack, seam, translation, technical specification, publication note, teaching route, or other local object.

### 4. Contract freeze

Declare the active rule-set, source boundary, authority boundary, evidence rules, missingness policy, closure set, thresholds, tolerances, return domain, seam policy, and publication boundary where applicable.

### 5. Minimal evidence or derivation path

Provide only the evidence needed for the declared claims to stand honestly. Do not use breadth to conceal an unclosed burden.

### 6. Canon read

Read the object through the active terms: drift, fidelity, roughness, return, integrity, and missingness. Add entropy, log-integrity, or other kernel outputs only when the work activates them.

### 7. Closures

Apply only declared gates, thresholds, tolerances, return domains, and closure logic. Diagnostics remain diagnostics unless explicitly adopted as gates.

### 8. Decisive witness

Run at least one decisive example, case, proof object, dataset, passage, or failure witness appropriate to the paper's role.

### 9. Missingness and boundary conditions

State what is unavailable, ambiguous, excluded, unresolved, source-limited, authority-limited, publication-limited, or repairable. State what the paper does not claim.

### 10. Return, ledger, and seam

Activate only when the paper claims return or continuity. No finite return means no return credit. No residual closure means no welded continuity.

### 11. Derived stance

Activate only when evaluation is part of the paper's contract. Derive stance after closure and missingness. A non-evaluative paper must not manufacture a stance for rhetorical completeness.

### 12. Readback and corpus relation

State what the work demonstrates, what it does not demonstrate, which source burdens remain local, what it updates or leaves unchanged, and what repair or next route remains available.

## 22. Order rule

> Object before conclusion. Contract before judgment. Closure before credit. Ledger before continuity. Stance last.

# IX. Boundary Discipline

## 23. Required boundaries

| Boundary | Governing question |
|---|---|
| **Object boundary** | What exact object is being written, evaluated, repaired, or published? |
| **Source boundary** | Which sources are admitted, and what burden does each actually carry? |
| **Authority boundary** | Which layer may define, execute, validate, translate, repair, or govern the claim? |
| **Interpretive boundary** | What meaning may be drawn without overclaiming? |
| **Evidence boundary** | What kind of support is required for the claim? |
| **Code boundary** | Which executable artifacts remain governed by repository, version, license, dependency, validator, and run-record conditions? |
| **Version boundary** | Which artifact state is active, and what changed from prior states? |
| **Publication boundary** | Which title, author, date, version, DOI if assigned, license, extent, citation form, and release status are active? |

## 24. Source-role rule

A source may be rich and still enter under one primary burden. Use sources as witnesses after the structure has been declared.

The preferred sequence is:

```text
Concept -> Case witness -> Source work
```

Do not silently promote:

- a pass paper into the whole theory;
- a closure into domain-native science;
- a casepack into universal proof;
- a technical reference into field declaration;
- a teaching architecture into proof of learning efficacy;
- a publication protocol into proof of local claims;
- a DOI record into stance;
- *Summa Reditus* into automatic local conformance.

## 25. Code and prose rule

Prose may describe executable protocol, but it does not become the executable artifact. Code may implement a route, but implementation does not redefine the object, field, theory, or Tier-1 kernel.

When code, validators, datasets, manifests, or repositories are cited, record their own version, license, dependency, source, and run conditions.

## 26. Boundary-change rule

If the object, contract, source set, evidence rule, authority layer, measurement interface, code boundary, version identity, or publication role changes, declare the seam.

Similarity does not preserve continuity. Declared repair makes continuity evaluable.

# X. Missingness

## 27. Missingness classes

Classify missingness before deriving stance.

| Class | Meaning | Default consequence |
|---|---|---|
| **Non-blocking** | The gap does not prevent the active burden from closing. | Record it; evaluation may continue. |
| **Evaluability-blocking** | Required structure for a verdict is unavailable. | NON_EVALUABLE. |
| **Contract-violating** | The active contract requires material that is demonstrably absent or invalid. | NONCONFORMANT only when the violation is established under a declared gate. |
| **Authority-boundary** | The source or operator lacks authority to define, adopt, or promote the object. | Block the affected claim; preserve the object at its actual status. |
| **Source-boundary** | Required evidence falls outside the admitted source set or a cited source cannot carry the burden assigned to it. | Repair the source boundary or narrow the claim. |
| **Publication-boundary** | Version, DOI, license, extent, citation, or release status is missing or inconsistent. | Block the affected publication claim; do not infer local proof failure. |
| **Repairable** | A declared action can restore evaluability without concealing a changed object. | State the repair path and whether a new contract or seam is required. |

## 28. Missingness rule

NON_EVALUABLE is not a softer form of NONCONFORMANT. It is the correct stance when the structure required for judgment is missing.

Every blocking gap must be returned with:

```text
Missing structure: [declare]
Missingness class: [declare]
Affected burden: [declare]
Current stance effect: [declare]
Repair path: [declare]
New seam required: [yes/no and why]
```

# XI. Drafting Passes

## 29. Pass 0 - Contract receipt

Complete the publication contract, mandate block, role assignment, claim budget, source map, authority map, evidence plan, missingness policy, and repair route.

## 30. Pass 1 - Skeleton

Write only:

- title candidate;
- status and publication boundary;
- mandate block;
- section headings in burden order;
- one sentence under each heading;
- primary claim and decisive failure mode.

## 31. Pass 2 - Core prose

Write the local argument plainly. Preserve the distinction between description, evidence, proof, computation, closure, and stance.

## 32. Pass 3 - Contract and authority discipline

Insert and verify:

- object and scope freeze;
- exclusions and non-goals;
- authority and functional axes;
- reserved-symbol discipline;
- source and evidence boundaries;
- active closures and thresholds;
- missingness policy;
- return and seam conditions where active;
- publication and version boundary.

## 33. Pass 4 - Evidence and operational discipline

Match every material claim to its required support. Reproduce computations where required. Verify validators and run records. Do not convert an illustrative example into a universal conclusion.

## 34. Pass 5 - Reader discipline

Test whether a serious reader can identify by the opening pages:

- what the work is;
- why it exists;
- what role it serves;
- what is fixed and what is local;
- what it claims and refuses;
- what sources and boundaries govern it;
- what would make the burden fail;
- where the reader can go next.

## 35. Pass 6 - Compression

Remove material that does not serve the primary burden. Relocate extended proof, inventory, history, data, or code into appendices or linked artifacts when the main claim remains locally complete without them.

## 36. Pass 7 - Audit and repair note

Run the protocol audits, classify missingness, derive protocol stance, and record every correction, expansion, compression, relocation, boundary repair, seam declaration, technical repair, transmission repair, or publication event.

No full polish precedes scope and boundary discipline.

# XII. Manuscript Surface Standard

The internal burden architecture governs meaning. This section governs how that burden is rendered as a readable paper. Venue rules may alter surface form but may not weaken the underlying contract.

## 37. Abstract

Unless a venue imposes another form, use a compact abstract that performs five moves in burden order:

1. name the object or problem;
2. state the gap, pressure, or unresolved burden;
3. identify the method, contract, or route used;
4. state the principal result at the level actually supported;
5. state the decisive limit, boundary, or corpus relation when needed to prevent overclaiming.

A 150-250 word abstract is a drafting default, not a protocol gate. Avoid citations, undefined acronyms, ceremonial corpus history, and claims stronger than the paper establishes. Do not announce CONFORMANT, proof, closure, or return in the abstract unless the manuscript actually derives it.

## 38. Introduction

The opening pages must let a serious reader recover:

- what exact object the paper addresses;
- why that object matters under the paper's declared role;
- what problem or burden is unresolved;
- the primary claim;
- the mandate and non-goal;
- what is new here and what is inherited;
- the source, authority, and evidence boundaries;
- the decisive contribution or result;
- the paper's route.

Do not begin with a universal history of the corpus when a local object is sufficient. Introduce only enough canon to make the local burden legible.

## 39. Prose and argument

Write in burden-bearing prose.

- Prefer direct claims over ceremonial or promotional language.
- Distinguish `defines`, `assumes`, `derives`, `measures`, `shows`, `supports`, `suggests`, and `illustrates`.
- Do not use rhetorical certainty to substitute for closure.
- Keep metaphor, analogy, and etymology outside proof burden unless the paper itself evaluates them.
- State assumptions before relying on them.
- Put qualifications beside the claim they constrain rather than hiding them in a late disclaimer.
- Remove repetition once a definition, boundary, or result is stable.
- Preserve technical terms exactly when paraphrase would change authority or meaning.

A reader should be able to tell at sentence level whether a passage is definition, interpretation, evidence, derivation, proof, diagnosis, or publication guidance.

## 40. Definitions and formal statements

Use theorem-like forms only when they carry a genuine formal burden.

- **Definition:** introduces a term, object, domain, map, or condition. A definition does not prove existence, usefulness, or truth.
- **Lemma:** proves a supporting result used later.
- **Proposition:** states a proved formal result whose burden is local but substantive.
- **Theorem:** reserves attention for a primary formal result supported by explicit assumptions and proof.
- **Corollary:** follows directly from an already established result.
- **Conjecture:** states an unresolved formal candidate and may not be written as established.
- **Claim:** may summarize a bounded assertion but may not hide the absence of proof required by its burden.
- **Example / Case / Witness:** demonstrates an instance, not universality.
- **Counterexample / Failure witness:** establishes a limit or defeats a universal claim within its admitted scope.

Every formal statement must expose its assumptions, domain, and dependency on local versus inherited structure. Use `Proof` only for an actual proof. A derivation, numerical verification, simulation, or case witness must retain its own type.

## 41. Notation and equations

Introduce notation before or at first use.

- Reserved Tier-1 symbols retain their fixed meanings.
- Local symbols must be declared with role, domain, and units or dimensional status where applicable.
- Do not reuse a symbol for a new meaning merely because a section is local.
- Distinguish exact identities from definitions, approximations, estimators, inequalities, diagnostics, and gates.
- Name the source or authority of any threshold, tolerance, normalization, or estimator that affects closure.
- Every displayed equation must contribute to the declared burden or to reproducibility.
- Check units, sign convention, normalization, and limiting behavior where those checks are meaningful.
- An equation that is true by definition may verify bookkeeping, but it may not be presented as independent empirical or closure evidence.

If notation becomes dense enough that a reader cannot recover it locally, include a notation table or glossary block.

## 42. Sources and citations

Citation follows burden.

- Prefer the closest primary source capable of carrying the claim.
- Cite a local witness as a local witness; do not let citation proximity promote its authority.
- Distinguish canon-facing sources, technical specifications, case evidence, datasets, implementations, commentary, and publication metadata.
- A citation supports only the claim actually borne by the cited source.
- Do not use citation count, DOI presence, repository existence, or repeated reuse as evidence of truth or conformance.
- Record versions for sources whose content, formulas, code, data, or status can change.
- Quote only when wording itself is material; otherwise paraphrase faithfully and preserve the source role.
- Priority language such as `first`, `only`, `complete`, or `definitive` requires an explicit evidence boundary.

Reference style may follow the target venue. Source-role discipline may not.

## 43. Figures and tables

Every figure or table must have one declared job: explain structure, report evidence, compare states, expose a failure, preserve provenance, or support reproduction.

A self-contained caption should identify, as applicable:

- the object shown;
- variables and units;
- source or dataset;
- contract or run state;
- normalization or transformation;
- uncertainty or missingness;
- the intended interpretation;
- the limit of that interpretation.

Do not let a visualization imply a gate that the contract never declared. Do not encode missingness as zero, smooth away a failed boundary, or use decorative complexity as evidence. If a figure is not needed to understand or test the burden, remove it or move it to supplementary material.

## 44. Related work and priority claims

Related work is a boundary map, not a prestige ritual.

Organize comparison by object, burden, method, authority, or evidence relation. State whether a source is antecedent, parallel, contrasting, enabling, critical, or merely adjacent.

Similarity does not establish lineage, equivalence, derivation, or priority. Claims of novelty or priority must be scoped to the searched source boundary and written no more strongly than that search permits.

Do not force external work into Structura Reditus terminology when the source does not make that identification. Translation is a local act and must be marked as such.

## 45. Discussion and limitations

Discussion interprets results after the evidentiary burden is visible.

Separate:

1. what the paper established;
2. what the result means under the active interpretation boundary;
3. what remains uncertain, missing, local, or unresolved;
4. what would falsify, weaken, or recontract the claim;
5. what may transfer to another domain and what requires a new closure.

Limitations are structural information, not ceremonial modesty. Classify blocking gaps using the missingness grammar. Do not invent new thresholds, explanations, or authority in the discussion to rescue a result that failed under the frozen contract.

## 46. Conclusion

The conclusion may compress but may not expand the burden.

It should return the reader to:

- the primary claim;
- the decisive result or witness;
- the decisive limitation or boundary;
- the paper's corpus relation;
- the next valid route, repair path, or open burden where one exists.

Do not introduce new evidence, new formal claims, new gates, or a stronger stance in the conclusion.

## 47. Venue, preprint, and supplement adaptation

External venue requirements may change title length, abstract length, heading names, reference style, section order, figure placement, appendix location, or word count. They do not change the paper's object, source roles, authority boundaries, evidence burden, missingness, closures, or stance logic.

When compression is required:

1. preserve the local burden in the main text;
2. move reproducibility depth, extended proof, data, code, and inventories to appendices or supplements;
3. leave a visible route from the main text to every relocated burden;
4. do not omit a decisive assumption, failure condition, or boundary merely to satisfy length;
5. record substantive differences between preprint, accepted manuscript, repository edition, and later revision as version changes, and declare a seam when the governing object or contract changes.

A venue may format the paper. It may not silently recontract it.

# XIII. Main Body and Appendix Discipline

## 48. Main body

The main body must contain the material required for the declared local burden to stand without live author mediation:

- purpose and role;
- minimum canon passport;
- local object and contract;
- source, authority, and evidence boundaries;
- minimal derivation or evidence path;
- decisive witness;
- failure or boundary condition;
- missingness and repair path;
- derived stance when evaluation is active;
- corpus relation and publication boundary.

## 49. Appendices

Appendices may contain:

- exhaustive proofs;
- extended derivations;
- full data tables;
- lineage and historical detail;
- case inventories;
- code paths and schemas;
- run records and manifests;
- supplementary figures;
- extended glossary or index material.

An appendix may deepen a claim. It may not hide a burden that the main body needs in order to remain intelligible or honest.

> The main body establishes that the local work stands. The appendices preserve how far its evidence and implementation reach.

# XIV. Publication Audits and Closure

## 50. Audit A - Object and contract

- Is the object named and versioned?
- Is one primary role declared?
- Are mandate, non-goal, reader result, and primary claim stable?
- Are scope and exclusions explicit?
- Are closure rules known before judgment?
- Are active thresholds and tolerances declared rather than inferred?

## 51. Audit B - Canon and hierarchy

- Is Reditus the object, Structura Reditus the field, and GCD the foundational theory?
- Is *Summa Reditus* correctly placed as the completed canon-facing treatise?
- Are local works treated as witnesses under local burdens?
- Are authority and function kept orthogonal?
- Are Tier-1 meanings and exact identities preserved?
- Is corpus-level return distinguished from local proof?

## 52. Audit C - Source, authority, and evidence

- Can each source carry the claim assigned to it?
- Does evidence type match claim type?
- Are code, data, repository, and prose boundaries preserved?
- Has any candidate, diagnostic, translation, validator, or implementation habit been silently promoted?
- Is publication status kept distinct from proof?

## 53. Audit D - Operational burden

Activate only where evaluation, closure, return, seam, or computation is claimed.

- Is the contract frozen before the run?
- Is the bounded trace admitted under the declared adapter?
- Are weights, normalization, guard band, and roughness convention named?
- Are diagnostics distinct from gates?
- Are return outcomes typed?
- Is return credit zero when return is infinite recurrence?
- Does the seam ledger reconcile within the declared tolerance?
- Are regime and stance distinct?
- Is stance derived only after closure and missingness checks?
